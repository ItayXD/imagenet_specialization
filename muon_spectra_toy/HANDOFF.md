# Toy models for width-consistency of weight spectra under Muon

We are looking for a simple, solvable model that gives different predictions for GD and Muon and explains Itay's observations on the second conv of ResNet18's first block (flattened 3·C_in × 3·C_out), trained on ImageNet and Cifar5M across widths: SGD roughly preserves the MP bulk and pulls out width-consistent spikes but shifts the bulk when the network is too narrow; Adam shifts the bulk; Muon yields spectra that look drawn from one spectral law at all widths, with outlier-fraction-vs-P curves collapsing across widths. Muon is idealized throughout as momentum-free with exact orthogonalization. Relevant tools (not prescriptions): the Lauditi–Pehlevan–Bordelon two-level DMFT, especially its ultra-wide regime, where finite-width direction statistics can be ignored and a bulk shift might be obtainable even with low-dimensional output; Rosenow-style extensive-rank BBP; possibly Saad–Solla. Linear networks may be too naive since the spectrum converges to the teacher's; with a low-rank teacher a bulk should persist under SGD.

Preliminary simulations live in `muon_spectra_toy/` (`width_spectra_toy.py`, numpy, single seed, loss-matched snapshots, MP-normalized plots following `scripts/plot_resnet_block_spectra.py`). Deep linear networks produce no bulk effects for either optimizer (gradients have rank ≤ C), but show one clean difference: GD spike positions encode the teacher's singular values while Muon's spikes move as a near-degenerate cluster at uniform speed. A 3-layer ReLU network on a low-dimensional multi-index target is more promising: GD keeps the bulk on MP at all widths with a few width-scattered spikes, while Muon visibly restructures the bulk (mass toward zero, shoulder past the MP edge, many edge-crossers) in a roughly width-consistent way. Caveats: single seed, ad-hoc learning rates, and at N=1024 the batch (B=512) caps the per-step update rank, producing a mild width drift at small σ. These readings have not been validated by Itay.

The forward path is to pick one solvable formulation and test whether it actually closes. The leading candidate is the ultra-wide DMFT with Muon inserted exactly: a rank-≤B gradient G=[g][φ]ᵀ has msign(G)=[g]·M(Q_g,Q_φ)·[φ]ᵀ with M a small function of Gram matrices that concentrate to DMFT kernels, so Muon becomes GD with kernel-whitened spike strengths (every direction enters at norm exactly η); the open question — and the crux — is whether the self-consistency loop still closes once the function-space feedback is whitened too. A cheaper static alternative is a Rosenow-style ensemble W(0) + η Σ_{s≤S} u_s v_sᵀ with S = BT growing extensive: equal strengths η for Muon versus a kernel-induced strength distribution for GD, with the spectrum from extensive-spike BBP. Saad–Solla with B=1 (where Muon is doubly-normalized SGD) closes but cannot restructure a bulk. No choice among these has been made yet; do not frame any of this in μP-vs-SP terms.
